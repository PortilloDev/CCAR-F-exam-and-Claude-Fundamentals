### Report structure (three mandatory sections)

**Report: "AI Impact on Creative Industries"**

1. **Well Established** — topics with full coverage and overlapping sources (cite sources).
2. **Contested** — topics where sources disagree; present both values, suggest why they differ; do NOT pick a winner.
3. **Coverage Gaps** — topics planned but not covered; list sources attempted, number of attempts, and what conclusions cannot be drawn as a result.

---

### Format preservation

| Original data format | Correct report format | Why |
|---------------------|----------------------|-----|
| Financial table | Table | Loses column-unit relationships if flattened |
| Structured list | Ordered list | Sequence is meaningful |
| News prose | Full sentences | Tone and quality lost if shortened to bullets |
| Technical sequence | Numbered steps or code | Hard to follow as a paragraph |

**Rule:** converting everything to one flat format loses information that was encoded in the original structure.

---

### Synthesis vs. formatting step

| Step | What it does | What it does NOT do |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Synthesis** | Merge overlapping claims; flag conflicts; identify gaps | Add new sources; verify claims |
| **Formatting** | Polish structure; choose correct format per content type | Change or overstate claims |

**Exam rule:** never overstate a claim in the formatting step. The formatting step only changes the presentation, not the content or certainty level.

---

### Universal rule for agentic tasks

Any task step not yet reviewed by a human should always carry in its output:
1. What it found (results).
2. What it could not find (gaps).
3. Any contradictions detected.

Never silently drop uncertainty — make it visible for the next step or the human reviewer.
